# Content Writing Guide

How to write pages and posts that rank on Google and get cited by AI search.

## Start from search intent

Every page answers one intent. Know it before writing a word.

| Intent | The searcher wants | The page must |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Book | To book now | Show the service, the price, and a booking button above the fold |
| Compare | To choose between options | Compare honestly, with prices and differences |
| Learn | To understand a treatment | Explain clearly, answer the real questions |
| Find near me | A local option | Name the city and area, show the address and map |

One page, one intent, one primary keyword plus its natural variations. A page trying to rank for everything ranks for nothing.

## Structure every page the same way

1. **Title:** service plus city, under 60 characters. Example: "Botox in Fort Worth | [Business]".
2. **Direct answer first:** 40 to 60 words answering the page's main question, right under the heading. This is the part Google snippets and AI engines lift.
3. **Detail:** the full explanation, in short sections with clear headings.
4. **Proof:** reviews, numbers, credentials, before and after results. Real proof, not adjectives.
5. **One call to action:** book, call, or ask a question. One, not three.
6. **FAQ:** 3 to 5 real questions with short direct answers, marked up with FAQPage schema.

## Writing rules

- **Short sentences, short paragraphs.** One idea per paragraph. No paragraph over 4 lines.
- **Facts over adjectives.** "4.9 stars from 636 Google reviews" beats "the best med spa in town."
- **Name the city naturally.** Real local detail (neighborhood, nearby landmark, local events) beats repeating "Fort Worth" ten times.
- **No keyword stuffing.** If a sentence sounds written for Google instead of a person, rewrite it.
- **No invented facts.** Prices, review counts, years in business, and credentials must be verified on the business's own pages or profiles.
- **Write for the client, not for SEO.** The reader is deciding whether to trust this business with their face, their health, or their money. Earn that trust.
- **Update, do not just publish.** A service page updated this month outranks a blog post from two years ago.

## Content types

### Service page

One treatment, one page. What it is, who it suits, what it costs (or the starting price), what to expect, how long it takes, aftercare, FAQ, booking button. Example: "Lip Filler in Fort Worth."

### Local landing page

One city or neighborhood, one page. Real local detail only: the area served, the address, parking, nearby landmarks, local reviews. Never clone the same page with only the city name changed.

### Blog post

One question, one answer, done well. Comparison posts ("Botox vs Dysport"), explainer posts ("How long does lip filler last?"), and local guides ("What to know before your first HydraFacial in Fort Worth") earn the most search and AI citations.

### FAQ content

Real questions from real clients. Short direct answers first, detail after. Mark up with FAQPage schema. FAQ content is the cheapest AEO and GEO win available.

## Before publishing

- [ ] Title under 60 characters, names the service and city.
- [ ] Meta description under 160 characters, names the benefit and the call to action.
- [ ] Direct answer (40 to 60 words) under the main heading.
- [ ] Headings in a logical order, one H1 only.
- [ ] Every fact verified: prices, numbers, names, credentials.
- [ ] One clear call to action.
- [ ] Internal links to related service pages.
- [ ] Images with descriptive file names and alt text.
- [ ] FAQ schema where the page has real questions and answers.
- [ ] Read it aloud once. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it like a person explaining it to a friend.
