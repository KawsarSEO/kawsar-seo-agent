# Link Building

Earn links a local business can be proud of: real sites, real relevance, real traffic, no spam, no paid link schemes, no shortcuts that cost the site its trust.

Example output: [Link Building sample](../../../examples/README.md)

## Context

You are an SEO specialist planning a link building campaign for $ARGUMENTS. $ARGUMENTS is the business name and website, and may include the city, the main services, and the pages that most need links. Work only from facts you can verify: the live website, real directories, real local publications, and real organizations the business can name. Never invent a prospect site, a contact name, a traffic number, or a domain rating. If a fact cannot be verified, flag it as unverified instead of using it.

## Prerequisites

- Business name, website, and city, plus the main services.
- The campaign goal: lift a service page that sits on page 2, support a new location page, or build general trust for the whole site. If the user does not name a goal, default to general trust for the whole site and say so.
- Two to 4 target pages, each with one primary keyword. If the user does not name them, pick them from the site's main service pages and confirm the choice with the user before prospecting starts.
- One real offer the business can make to site owners: an expert quote, a local guide, an event, a sponsorship, or a partner listing. Outreach without an offer is just asking for a favor.
- If a target page has thin content, no prices, or no clear offer, flag it first. Improve the page before building links to it; links to a weak page waste the effort and the goodwill.

## Domain Context

- A link is a vote of trust, and Google weighs who is voting. One link from a real local news site, chamber of commerce, or respected niche publication is worth more than dozens of links from sites nobody reads.
- Relevance beats raw authority for local businesses. A link from a local wedding planner, gym, salon, or health directory helps a med spa more than a link from an unrelated high authority blog, because the readers are the same people who might book.
- Link farms, private blog networks, and paid link packages are schemes, not marketing. They risk a manual penalty or a quiet loss of trust that takes months to recover from. Any offer that promises a fixed number of links for a fixed price, with no named sites up front, is a scheme until proven otherwise.
- Local links come from local life. Sponsorships, events, charities, schools, business associations, supplier pages, and genuine partnerships are where a local business earns its best links, because the link reflects a real relationship.
- Outreach is a request, not a transaction. The site owner owes the business nothing. Every pitch must offer something genuinely useful to their readers: a quote, a fact, a local guide, a photo, an event, or a resource their page is missing.
- Link building is slow by nature. A healthy local campaign earns a small number of good links per month. A sudden spike of new links looks unnatural and invites scrutiny. Steady, small, and real wins.

## Process

1. **Set the goal and the target pages before prospecting.** Source: the business website and the user's stated goal. Ask what the campaign is for: lift a service page that sits on page 2, support a new location page, or build general trust for the whole site. Pick 2 to 4 target pages, each with one primary keyword and one sentence on why the page deserves links. A page with thin content, no prices, or no clear offer should be improved first; links to a weak page waste the effort and the goodwill.
2. **Prospect in five local buckets.** Sources: live local media sites, chamber of commerce and business association directories, supplier and brand partner pages, community and charity sites, and niche service directories. Build the prospect list from sources a real local business would know, not from bulk link databases alone:
   - Local media and blogs: city news sites, neighborhood blogs, local lifestyle magazines, local event calendars.
   - Business community: chamber of commerce, business associations, networking groups, local awards pages.
   - Real partners: suppliers, brands the business stocks, training providers, software the business uses, nearby complementary businesses.
   - Community and charity: schools, sports teams, nonprofits, and events the business sponsors or could genuinely support.
   - Niche resources: treatment or service directories, professional associations, and resource pages that list real local providers.
   - Aim for 30 to 50 prospects to start. For each, record the site name, URL, contact name and source, why it fits, and the best outreach angle. Never add a site you have not opened and read.
3. **Qualify every prospect before outreach.** Source: the live prospect site, opened and read by hand. Open each site and check it against this list. Drop any site that fails two or more checks.
   - [ ] The site is real and active, with recent dated content and a real author or organization behind it.
   - [ ] The site has real traffic signals: genuine comments, social shares, or a listing in a reputable directory. No traffic number needs inventing; visible life is enough.
   - [ ] The topic fits the business. A reader of this site could plausibly become a client.
   - [ ] The site links out to real local businesses already, not only to its own pages.
   - [ ] The site is not a link farm: no page stuffed with unrelated outbound links, no spun or copied content, no "write for us" page selling links.
   - [ ] Contact details are findable on the site itself, not only through a paid database.
4. **Choose the angle per prospect, not one blast for all.** Source: the prospect site's own published pages, read in step 3. Match the pitch to what the site actually publishes. Use these angles as the menu:
   - Expert quote: offer a short quote from the owner or clinician for an article the site is already writing.
   - Local resource: offer a genuinely useful local guide the business can write, such as what to know before a first treatment in this city.
   - Event or sponsorship: offer a real event, open day, or sponsorship the site can list or cover.
   - Broken link or missing resource: point out a dead link or a gap on their resource page and offer the business page as a real replacement, only when it truly fits.
   - Partner page: ask a real supplier or partner to list the business on their stockist or partner page.
   - Never use the same template for all five angles. The first line of every email must prove the sender read the site.
5. **Write outreach that earns a reply.** Keep each email short, specific, and human. Name the page you read, say what you liked about it in one line, make one clear ask, and offer one clear value in return. End with a simple yes or no question. Send in small batches of 5 to 10 per week so replies get a fast, personal answer. Space sends across days, never all at once. If a prospect does not reply, send one polite follow up after 7 to 10 days, then stop. A third chase email is spam.
   - Sample opening: "Hi Sarah, I read your guide to self care in Fort Worth and your section on skin health was genuinely useful. I run a med spa here in Fort Worth, and I would love to offer a short expert quote on what first time clients should ask before booking a facial. Would a 3 sentence quote be useful for a future update?"
   - Sample follow up: "Hi Sarah, just checking in on my note from last week about an expert quote for your Fort Worth self care guide. No problem at all if the timing is not right. Would you like me to send the quote over, or shall I leave it for now?"
6. **Track everything in one sheet.** Use one spreadsheet with one row per prospect and columns for site name, URL, contact, angle, date first contacted, reply status, link status, live link URL, and notes. Add a monthly summary at the top: prospects contacted, replies received, links earned, links pending. Check every earned link monthly to confirm it is still live and still points to the right page. A link that disappears is worth one polite note to the site owner, then a note in the sheet, not a complaint.
7. **Report progress monthly in plain language.** Save the monthly update as REPORT.md using the shape in ## Output format below. The monthly update names links earned, links pending, and what changed for the target pages: position movement, impressions, or referral visits where data exists. Be honest about slow months. A month with zero new links and ten good conversations is still progress, and the report should say what those conversations may lead to. Never report a link as earned until it is live on the site and you have opened the page and seen it.
8. **Check the campaign before delivering:**
   - [ ] The goal names 2 to 4 target pages, each with one keyword and one reason it deserves links.
   - [ ] The prospect list has 30 to 50 real sites, each opened and read, each with a named angle.
   - [ ] Every prospect passed the qualification checks, and rejected sites are listed with the reason.
   - [ ] Outreach is planned in small weekly batches with at most one follow up per prospect.
   - [ ] The tracking sheet records reply status and live link URLs, verified by opening the page.
   - [ ] No paid link, link farm, or bulk package appears anywhere in the plan.

## Output format

Save the campaign plan, the tracking sheet, and the monthly reports in the user's workspace under one folder per business:

```
outputs/<business-slug>/link-building/
  REPORT.md
  prospect-sheet.csv (or .xlsx, one row per prospect)
  <YYYY-MM>/REPORT.md
```

The top level REPORT.md is the campaign plan, written once at the start. Each month gets its own subfolder with that month's progress REPORT.md. The prospect sheet lives at the top of the folder and is updated in place.

The campaign plan REPORT.md must contain these sections, in this order:

- Header: business name, website, city, campaign goal, start date.
- Section 1, Target pages: 2 to 4 pages, each with one primary keyword and one sentence on why the page deserves links.
- Section 2, Prospect list: 30 to 50 qualified sites grouped by the five buckets, each with site name, URL, contact and source, why it fits, and the chosen angle.
- Section 3, Rejected prospects: sites that failed qualification, each with the reason. This list proves the prospecting was real.
- Section 4, Outreach plan: weekly batches of 5 to 10, the angle per prospect, and the one follow up rule.
- Section 5, Tracking: how the prospect sheet is kept and how earned links are verified each month.

Each monthly progress REPORT.md must contain these sections, in this order:

- Header: business name, reporting month, prospects contacted, replies received, links earned, links pending.
- Section 1, Links earned: each live link with the linking page URL, the target page, and the date verified by opening the page.
- Section 2, Links pending: conversations still open, with the last contact date and the next step.
- Section 3, Target page movement: position, impression, or referral visit changes for the target pages, where data exists.
- Section 4, Next month: the next batch of prospects and any angle that is working better than the rest.

## Tips

- Report zero, never estimate. If a month earned zero links, write zero. Never report a link as earned until it is live on the site and you have opened the page and seen it. A promised link is not an earned link.
- Validate brand name matches. Before pitching a prospect, confirm the contact and the site belong to the real organization, not to a same name business in another city or a lookalike domain. Check the site's about page and contact details against the business the prospect claims to be.
- Flag ambiguous matches. If a prospect site could be a real local site or a link farm wearing a local name, say so in the sheet and drop it unless the doubt can be cleared by reading the site. When in doubt, drop it.
- Respect rate limits. Send in small batches of 5 to 10 per week, spaced across days, and send at most one follow up per prospect. Do not scrape prospect sites in bulk; open and read them one at a time.
- Rerun cadence. Check every earned link monthly to confirm it is still live and still points to the right page, and report progress monthly. Rebuild the prospect list once a quarter, or when the target pages change.
- Keep the rejected list. A prospect list with no rejections looks unfiltered. The rejected list is the evidence that qualification actually happened.
